From here on, the method is simple: **as many hours of Japanese as you can, of things you enjoy.** A few things help along the way.

## Keep the grammar going

Work through the **N4** lessons on the [grammar page](/grammar.html) at your own pace: [〜てしまう](/grammar.html#te-shimau), [〜たら](/grammar.html#tara), [〜そう](/grammar.html#sou-looks), [〜のに](/grammar.html#noni)… You'll notice them underlined more and more often in what you watch. After N4, carry on with **N3** ([〜わけだ](/grammar.html#wake), [〜はず](/grammar.html#hazu), [〜ところ](/grammar.html#tokoro)…), then **N2** and **N1**. N2 and N1 grammar is mostly written and formal, so it shows up more in novels and the news than in anime. When something keeps confusing you, write your own lesson for it (see [Add your own lessons](/grammar.html#grammar-howto)).

## Make the switch

- **More Japanese, less English:** change your phone and games to Japanese, follow Japanese YouTubers and streamers, listen to Japanese [podcasts](/podcasts.html) (*Nihongo con Teppei* is a great first one).
- **Turn off the 2nd subtitle.** Then, later, try an episode with Japanese subtitles hidden (**H**).
- **Japanese explanations:** once you have about 5000 words, start reading definitions in a Japanese dictionary (国語辞典) now and then, instead of English ones.

## Speaking and writing

When you can follow a slice-of-life show without many lookups, try **shadowing** (repeating lines right after the audio), talking to yourself, writing a short diary, or a tutor or language exchange. You'll be surprised how much is already there from all the input.

> Track your hours in the [stats on the settings page](/settings.html#stats-section). Learners often say it "clicks" somewhere between 1000 and 1500 hours. Every one of them counts.

**Goals:** N4 grammar, 2000 known words, 300 hours. After that: keep going. 頑張って！
