From here on, the method is simple: **as many hours of English as you can, of things you enjoy.** A few things help along the way.

## Keep the grammar going

Work through **B1** on the [grammar page](/grammar.html) at your own pace: [second conditional](/grammar.html#second-conditional), [passive](/grammar.html#passive), [past perfect](/grammar.html#past-perfect), [present perfect continuous](/grammar.html#present-perfect-continuous)… then **B2** and **C1**. You'll see them underlined more and more in what you watch. When something keeps confusing you, write your own lesson for it (see [Add your own lessons](/grammar.html#grammar-howto)).

## Make the switch

- **More English, less of your language:** set your phone and games to English, follow American YouTubers and streamers, listen to English [podcasts](/podcasts.html) (*All Ears English* is popular with learners).
- **Drop the 2nd subtitle.** Later, try an episode with the English subtitles hidden too (**H**).
- **English-only definitions:** the popup already shows English definitions first. Try to understand words through them instead of translating.

## Speaking and writing

When you can follow a sitcom without many lookups, try **shadowing** (repeating lines right after the audio), talking to yourself, a short diary in English, or a tutor or language exchange. You'll be surprised how much is already there from all the input.

> Track your hours in the [stats on the settings page](/settings.html#stats-section). Many learners say it "clicks" somewhere after 1,000 hours. Every hour counts.

**Goals:** B1 grammar, 3,000 known words, 300 hours. After that: keep going!
