**AJATT** ("All Japanese All The Time") is a well-known way of learning Japanese, described by Khatzumoto: learn from huge amounts of real input instead of textbooks. It works for any language, so here it's **All English All The Time**.

1. **Input is what teaches you.** You pick up a language by hearing and reading lots of it that you *mostly* understand. Grammar and word lists are only there to make that input understandable sooner.
2. **Make English your environment.** Swap the things you already do (YouTube, shows, games, music, podcasts, your phone's language) for English versions, one at a time.
3. **Fun beats discipline.** Watch and read what you actually enjoy. If something bores you, drop it and find something else. You'll keep doing what's fun for years.
4. **Not understanding is normal.** At the start you'll understand little. That's fine. Comprehension grows slowly from "a few words" to "most of it". Don't stop to translate everything.
5. **A little SRS every day.** Anki makes words stick. Do your reviews every day, keep it short, and spend the rest of your time on English itself.
6. **Speaking comes later.** Output gets much easier after a lot of input. You don't need to force it at the start (but talk if you want to).

> It doesn't have to be all or nothing. Every extra hour of English helps. Start with what you can, and grow it.

## How this course works

The steps go from zero to watching comfortably. They overlap: you'll keep doing Anki while you immerse, and keep immersing while you learn grammar. Each step has a **when**, and some have goals the app checks for you. Press **mark as done** when you're ready to move on.
