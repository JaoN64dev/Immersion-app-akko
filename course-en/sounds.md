title: The sounds of English
summary: Get your ear used to American English, and stop guessing pronunciation from spelling.
when: weeks 1–2

English spelling often doesn't match the sound, so learning to **hear** it first makes everything after this easier.

- **Don't trust the spelling.** *though*, *through*, *tough* and *thought* all sound different. Always learn a new word together with its sound.
- **Sounds many learners mix up:** **l / r** (light / right), **th** (think, this), **v / b** (very / berry), **ship / sheep**, **cat / cut**.
- **Typical American sounds:** the **r** is always pronounced (car, water), and **t** between vowels sounds like a quick d (water, better, a lot of → "a lodda").
- **Weak forms:** small words are squeezed in natural speech: *going to* → "gonna", *want to* → "wanna", *and* → "n".
- A few short videos about **phonics** (how letters map to sounds) are a good start.

## On this app

- Click a word and the popup shows its **IPA** (pronunciation spelling) and a **🔊** button with an American recording. Listen to every new word once.
- When you mine a word to Anki, its recording goes on the card.

> You don't need a perfect accent. The goal is an ear that can tell the sounds apart.

**Done when:** you're used to checking how a new word sounds, and the main sound pairs don't confuse you anymore.
