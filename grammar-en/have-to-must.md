Both mean "it's necessary".

- **have to**: the most common in American English, often because of rules or circumstances: I **have to** work on Saturday. / She **has to** go.
- **must**: stronger, or written rules: You **must** wear a seatbelt.
- Casual: **have got to / gotta**: I'**ve got to** go.

> The negatives mean different things!
> **mustn't** = it's not allowed.
> **don't have to** = it's not necessary.

## Examples
- I have to get up early tomorrow.
- You mustn't smoke here.
- You don't have to come if you're busy.

## Quiz
- It's free. You ___ pay. = don't have to | mustn't | have to
- She ___ to wear a uniform at work. = has | have | must
